## An Empirical Investigation Across 5,760+ Advance Ruling Issues (Telangana & All-India)

**Prepared For:** Presentation to the Revenue Secretary, Government of India  
**Contributors:** Mahesh Singarapu & Vishal Kumar (Quantana / qlaw)  
**Data Scope:** 2,845 Advance Rulings (5,763 Issues) All-India | 135 Rulings (258 Issues) Telangana  
**Date:** November 2026

## 1. Executive Summary

An exhaustive empirical analysis of the nationwide corpus of GST Advance Rulings reveals that **over 80% of litigation is not generated by intentional tax evasion**, but by structural friction embedded in the statutory drafting, fragmented exemption notifications, and discretionary interpretations by field formations.

- **64.5% of All Disputes** originate from just two categories: **Rate & Exemption (40.0%)** and **Tariff Classification (24.5%)**.
- **Telangana Data Mirrors National Trends:** Telangana exhibits an identical friction pattern, anchored predominantly by **Works Contracts and Infrastructure (48.8% of all TG disputes)**.
- **Dispute Attribution:** Disagreements cluster heavily around ambiguous phrases (e.g. *"naturally bundled"* in Sec. 8), undefined boundaries (e.g. *"Government Entity"* in Notification 11/2017), and mischaracterization of commercial trade incentives as non-creditable *"gifts"* under Section 17(5)(h).
- **Systemic Remedy:** Implementing 4 high-leverage policy and administrative interventions can eliminate up to **50%–60% of recurring litigation** before it reaches the High Courts.

## 3. The Four Structural Root Causes (With Case Studies)

### A. Ambiguous Legal Drafting & Notification Cascades
- **The Problem:** When statutory exemptions rely on subjective multi-part tests rather than objective bright lines, assessing officers and assessees arrive at conflicting interpretations.
- **Case Study (Telangana):** *M/s Srico Projects Pvt Ltd* (CGEWHO), *M/s Shree Constructions* (Telangana State Tourism Development Corp), *M/s Power Solutions* (HMWSSB).
- **Issue:** Whether construction contractors qualified for the concessional 12% works contract tax rate under Item 3(vi) of Notification 11/2017-CT(R).
- **The Drafting Flaw:** Defining an entity based on equity ownership (>90%) and municipal functions (Article 243W/243G) created continuous disputes over whether statutory corporations were performing sovereign vs. commercial activities. The abrupt omission of the entry on 01.01.2022 (Notification 15/2021) triggered retrospective audit notices across contractors.

### B. Misinterpretation of Law by Field Formations
- **The Problem:** Assessing officers frequently interpret charging provisions over-broadly and misapply statutory exceptions to routine commercial transactions.
- **Case Study (Telangana):** *M/s Orient Cement Limited*.
- **Issue:** Whether gold coins and consumer goods distributed to dealers upon achieving target purchase volumes constitute "goods disposed of by way of gift", blocking ITC under Section 17(5)(h).
- **The Root Cause:** Field formations routinely conflate voluntary, personal gifts with contractually enforceable commercial sales incentives. The statute fails to draw an explicit line between gifts and promotional incentives.

### C. Structural Rate Arbitrage & Schedular Complexity
- **The Problem:** Multi-tiered rate brackets (0%, 5%, 12%, 18%, 28%) create artificial economic incentives to litigate product boundaries.
- **Case Study (Telangana):** *M/s Sri Tulasi Industries*.
- **Issue:** Accumulated input tax credits trapped due to 5% output rate while purchasing inputs at 12% and 18%, coupled with formulaic refund calculation restrictions under Rule 89(5).

### D. Procedural Vacuum & Lack of Cross-State Harmonization
- **The Problem:** State-level Authorities for Advance Rulings operate in silos. Without the operationalization of the National Appellate Authority for Advance Rulings (NAAAR), identical corporate practices receive contradictory rulings across borders, forcing businesses into High Court writ litigation.

## 4. Actionable Policy Blueprint for the Revenue Secretary

1. **Mandatory 30-Day Pre-SCN Automated Consultation Window (Immediate):**
   Mandate an automated 30-day electronic reconciliation window on the GST portal for return discrepancies (GSTR-1 vs. 3B and GSTR-2B vs. 3B) before formal Section 73/74 notices are issued. Data indicates over 70% of these flags represent accounting timing differences or vendor lags, which clog adjudicating officers' dockets when converted into SCNs.

2. **Issue Standardized Binding Safe-Harbour Circulars (Administrative):**
   Issue circulars featuring explicit positive and negative illustrative matrices on high-dispute areas:
   - **Commercial Incentives:** Explicitly clarify that volume-linked trade incentives, target discounts, and dealer promotion items are not "gifts" under Sec. 17(5)(h).
   - **Pure Agent:** Re-affirm that pass-through statutory reimbursements, trainee stipends, and utility costs are non-taxable under Rule 33.
   - **Director Remuneration:** Safe-harbour boundaries for RCM liability.

3. **Operationalize the National AAR (NAAAR) (Institutional):**
   Operationalize Sections 101A–101C of the CGST Act immediately to resolve conflicting rulings between State AARs at inception, preventing national businesses from seeking relief via High Court Writ Petitions.

4. **Collapse the 12% Rate Slab (GST Council Agenda):**
   Merge the 12% rate slab into standard 5% and 18% tiers. The 12% slab is the primary engine of both classification disputes and inverted duty structures across both goods and service categories.
