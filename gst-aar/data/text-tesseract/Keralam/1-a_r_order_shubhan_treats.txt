6.2.1 The first issue for consideration pertains to whether the supply of sweets,

sweets such as Bengali sweets (e.g., Rasgulla, Gulab Jamun), North Indian
sweets (e.g., Ghee Jalebi, Khalkhand), and dry fruit-based sweets (e.g. Kaju Katli,

6.2.2 As per Section 2(52) of the Central Goods and Services Tax (CGST) Act,
2017, “goods” are defined as every kind of movable property, subject to certain
exclusions. Likewise, Section 2(102) defines “services” as anything other than
goods, money, and securities, with certain inclusions. Accordingly, the items
mentioned in the application qualify as “goods” by default, since they are movable
properties and are not excluded from the statutory definition. They cannot be

treated as “services” unless explicitly provided for under the Act.

6.2.3 Schedule II, Entry 6 of the CGST Act provides that the composite supply of
goods being food or any other article for human consumption or any drink (other
than alcoholic liquor) by way of or as part of any service, or in any manner
whatsoever, for cash, deferred payment, or other valuable consideration, shall be
treated as a supply of service. Furthermore, Section 2(30) defines “composite
supply” as a supply made by a taxable person to a recipient, consisting of two or
more taxable supplies of goods or services or both, which are naturally bundled
and supplied in conjunction with each other in the ordinary course of business,
where one of the supplies is a principal supply. Therefore, while the items
mentioned are prima facie goods, if they are supplied in a manner that involves a

naturally bundled service component in the ordinary course of business, such



supply is to be treated as a supply of service in accordance with the provisions of

the Act.

6.2.4 The classification of a supply as either a supply of goods or a supply of
service depends significantly on how the supply is made and the presence of any
accompanying service element. The same item for instance, a sweet or savoury
can be supplied either as a standalone item (in packaged or loose form) or as part
of a broader offering involving preparation, presentation, and delivery for
immediate consumption. Where the supply is made over the counter without any
value added element such as serving, reheating, or preparation for consumption
on the premises or through home delivery, it retains its character as a supply of
goods. However, if the same item is supplied in a manner that involves
preparation for immediate consumption, presentation, packaging for service, or
delivery, particularly in the course of a dine-in, takeaway, or similar setup, it
assumes the character of a restaurant service, which is classifiable as a supply of
service under Schedule II of the CGST Act. This principle is equally applicable
when the items are sold through electronic commerce operators (ECOs). In such
cases, if the food items are prepared fresh, packed, and delivered as part of a
service-oriented offering resembling how a restaurant would operate- the supply
may be treated as a composite supply of service, with the service component (i.e.,
restaurant service) being the principal supply. On the other hand, if the sale is
limited to packaged food items, without any element of cooking, reheating, or
service by the supplier, it continues to be treated as a supply of goods. Hence, the
determination of whether the transaction is a supply of goods or a supply of
service depends entirely on the factual matrix and how the transaction is

executed.

6.2.5 This position finds support in various advance rulings and is consistent
with the GST Council's intent behind distinguishing between restaurant services
and trading in food products. Judicial and quasi-judicial authorities, such as the
Appellate Authority for

6.3. Questions on value of supplies

6.3.1 Under this, the first sub-question pertains to whether additional charges
for packing, special packaging boxes, and delivery are to be included in the value
of the supply of goods or services made by the applicant. As per Section 15 of the
Central Goods and Services Tax (CGST) Act, 2017, Sub-section (1) provides that
the value of a supply of goods or services or both shall be the transaction value,
which is the price actually paid or payable for the said supply, provided the
supplier and the recipient are not related and the price is the sole consideration
for the supply. Further, Section 15(2)(c) stipulates that the value of supply shall

include incidental expenses, including commission and packing, charged by the


11

supplier to the recipient. It also includes any amount charged for anything done
by the supplier in respect of the supply of goods or services or both at the time of,
or before, delivery of goods or supply of services. Accordingly, it is evident that
any additional charges such as those for packing, special packaging boxes, and
delivery, if incurred by the supplier in connection with the supply and forming
part of the price actually paid or payable, are required to be included in the value

of the supply in accordance with Section 15(2)(c) of the CGST Act, 2017.

6.4.3 The second sub-question pertains to the applicant’s eligibility for ITC on
restaurant services provided at their premises, in accordance with Notification
No. 11/2017-Central Tax (Rate). Unlike the first sub-question, this applies only to
services supplied by the applicant. With respect to the restaurant service
segment, Notification No. 46/2017-Central Tax (Rate) dated 14.11.2017 amended
the original entry under Notification No. 11/2017-Central Tax (Rate) to prescribe
a concessional rate of 5% GST (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST) for restaurant services,
subject to the condition that the supplier shall not avail input tax credit (ITC) on
goods and services used in supplying such service. This condition is not optional
but mandatory where the concessional rate is availed. Furthermore, Explanation
(iv) inserted in Notification No. 11/2017-CT(R) clarified that the restriction on ITC
includes: (a) a complete bar on availing credit for inputs and input services used
exclusively in such supplies; and (b) a requirement to reverse proportionate ITC
under Rule 42 of the CGST Rules for common inputs and input services used for
both restaurant service and other taxable supplies. Accordingly, in cases where
the applicant is paying GST at the 5% rate for restaurant services, as is
applicable to non-specified premises (i.e., establishments not operating from
hotels with room tariff exceeding %7,500 per unit per day), ITC cannot be availed
on any goods or services used for such restaurant activities. ITC is permitted only
where the restaurant is located in a specified premise and the applicable GST rate
is 18% (9% CGST + 9% SGST) without the ITC restriction. Since the applicant
does not fall under the category of specified premises and is liable to discharge

GST at the concessional rate of 5%, the condition of no ITC squarely applies.
