8. Coming to the discussion part, let us first discuss part of the notification
related to the issue at hand. Taxability in cases wherein 'premium on plots received
upfront in lumpsum amount by the GIDC: On perusal it is ample clear from the
entry no. 41 of the Not. no. 12/2017 Central Tax (Rate) dated 28.06.2017 that the
one time upfront premium paid towards long term (thirty years, or more) leasing out
of industrial plots is exempted from payment of GST.
9.
Further,
Taxability in cases wherein premium on plots received in
installments by the GIDC: From plain reading of entry no. 41 of the Not. no.
12/2017 Central Tax (Rate) dated 28.06.2017 (supra) it appears that only the one
Page 7 of 13

time upfront premium paid towards leasing out of industrial plots is exempted, as
there is no specific mention of 'payment in installments' and only the phrase 'one
time upfront premium' is used in the said exemption notification. Further, no
specific entry regarding exemption of 'premium on plots paid in installments' is
available in the said exemption Not. No. 12/2017 Central Tax (Rate) dated
28.06.2017.
However CBIC vide cicular no. 101/20/2019-GST dated 30.04.2019 clarified the
above issue as under:
"It is hereby clarified that GST exemption on the upfront amount (called as
premium, salami, cost, price, development charges or by any other name)
payable for long term lease (of thirty years, or more) of industrial plots or
plots for development of infrastructure for financial business under Entry No.
41 of Exemption Notification 12/2017 - Central Tax (R) dated 28.06.2017 is
admissible irrespective of whether such upfront amount is payable or paid in
one or more instalments, provided the amount is determined upfront."
Viewing above clarification issued by the CBIC it is clear that the premium on plots?
paid in installments towards long term lease (of thirty years, or more) of industrial
plots or plots for development of infrastructure for financial business is also
exempted provided the amount is determined upfront.

17. While arriving at taxability of interest/default interest, it is pertinent to
mention herein that two types of services/outward supplies are involved between the
applicant and the Lessee (industrial unit); first one is 'Allotment of Plot' and Second
one is "Granting of Leasehold rights'. However the principal supply in the
transaction is that of 'granting of leasehold rights' or 'the leasing service'. In
connection it may be noted that in the exemption notification 12/2017 (supra) also
the words used in the "description of service' while granting exemption to one time
upfront premium are 'leviable in respect of the service, by way of granting long
term (thirty years, or more) lease of industrial plots. Hence it appears that the
allotment of plot and granting of lesaehold rights is being treated as 'composite
service' and exemption is provided only to a portion of the abovementioned
composite supply. Hence the 'leasing service' being principal supply of the above
composite supply remains taxable and the value of taxable supply will be equal to
the transaction value as per Section 15(1) of the CGST Act, 2017 and any 'interest
or late fee or penalty for delayed payment of any consideration for any supply' shall
form part of the consideration /value of taxable supply as per Section 15(2)(d) of the
CGST Act, 2017.
18.
Though the law with reference to inclusion of interest in the value of 'taxable
supply' is clear from Section 15(2)(d) however there appears to be confusion
regarding taxability of additional/penal interest in the trade whether it would be
taxable or not?.
Page 10 of 13

19. In connection to the inclusion of additional /default interest as consideration
in the value of taxable supply, attention is invited to Case-1 mentioned at Para 3 in
CBIC Circular No. 102/21/2019-GST dated 28"June, 2019 on the subject
Clarification regarding applicability of GST on additional / penal interest?
(hereinafter referred to as 'said circular') detailed herein as under for ready refrence:
"Case - 1: X sells a mobile phone to Y. The cost of mobile phone is R$ 40,000/-.
However, X gives Y an option to pay in installments, Rs 11,000/- every month before 10th
day of the following month, over next four months (Rs 11,000/- *4 = Rs. 44,000/-). Further,
as per the contract, if there is any delay in payment by Y beyond the scheduled date,
Y would be liable to pay additional / penal interest amounting to Rs. 500/- per month for the
delay. In some instances, X is charging Y Rs. 40,000/- for the mobile and is separately
issuing another invoice for providing the services of extending loans to Y, the
consideration for which is the interest of 2.5% per month and an additional / penal
interest amounting to Rs. 500/- per month for each delay in payment.
20.
under:
In the said circular with refrence to Case-l, it is clarified by the Board as
"Case 1: As per the provisions of sub-clause (d) of sub-section (2) of
section 15 of the CGST Act, the amount of penal interest is to be included
in the value of supply. The transaction between X and Y is for supply of
taxable goods i.e. mobile phone. Accordingly, the penal interest would be
taxable as it would be included in the value of the mobile, irrespective of
the manner of invoicing.".
21.
Therefore in view of the provisions of Section 15(1) read with Section 15 2(d)
and Board's clarification vide circular dated 28.06.2019, all the transactions
mentioned at Para 16 above (except 'Land Premium' exempted vide Not.No.
12/2017), shall form part of the consideration for the principal supply ie. leasing
service.
22.
Further it may be noted that Schedule II Entry 5 (e) of CGST Act, 2017
includes the following as supply
"agreeing to the obligation to refrain from an act, or tolerate an act or a
Page 11 of 13

situation, or to do an act ".
23. As per above said Entry, the event of tolerating an act / situation of the
Lessee i.e non-payment of Land premium upfront; and allowing the Lessee to
pay in installments with interest @11% and further allowing the Lessee to pay
Installment cum interest after fixed due date with payment of default interest
@8%, is considered as 'supply'. The applicant is tolerating the abovesaid
act/situation of the Lessee upto a period of six months as per Regulation 24 of the
of the GIDC (Allotment, Transfer and Sub-Lease) Regulations, 2023 (supra)
after which leasehold rights are liable to be withdrawn.
24.
Therefore, going by the above discussed provisions of the CGST Act,
2017, clarification provided by the CBIC Circular and in absence of explicit
exemption of interest or/and additional interest charged on 'premium on plot, it
is clear that interest or/and additional/deafult interest levied on delayed payment
of 'premium on plots' treating it as consideration towards composite service of
'leasing service' is chargeable to tax and shall be taxed at the rate on which
principal supply 'leasing service' is taxed ie. 18%.
