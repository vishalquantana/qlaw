Judgments in Courts vs. Advance Rulings: Two Halves of the Same Coin

Why High Court and Supreme Court dockets diverge fundamentally from the AAR profile

Advance Rulings (The Front Door)
Flaws in Legislative Drafting

Strictly limited by Section 97(2) to 7 questions. Over 65% of issues focus on Rate, Exemption, and Tariff Classification as taxpayers seek prospective commercial certainty before entering deals.

Limitation: AAR cannot rule on Place of Supply, recovery notices, or statutory validity.
Court Judgments (The Back Door)
Excesses in Enforcement & Due Process

Over 60% of High Court writ petitions involve enforcement actions barred from AAR: Place of Supply (IGST vs CGST), Rule 86A credit ledger freezing, bank attachments (Sec. 83), and mechanical SCNs violating natural justice (Sec. 75(4)).

Substantive Overlap: Landmark battles like Safari Retreats (Sec 17(5)(d)) and VKC Footsteps (inverted refunds) escalate to Courts when AARs give split rulings.

The Four Structural Root Causes

Why assessees and revenue authorities are constantly locked in dispute

Root Cause 1

Ambiguous Drafting & Notification Cascades

Complex, frequently amended exemption notifications with vague subjective definitions force businesses into dispute.

Telangana Case: M/s Srico Projects & Shree Constructions
Unending litigation over whether CGEWHO and State Tourism Corp qualify as 'Government Entities' under Notification 11/2017 for 12% vs 18% works contract rate.
Root Cause 2

Misinterpretation by Field Formations

Assessing officers frequently treat pass-through reimbursements (Rule 33) as taxable turnover and invoke Schedule II as a charging section.

Telangana Case: M/s Orient Cement Ltd
Department attempted to block ITC under Sec 17(5)(h) by mischaracterizing commercial target volume incentives (gold coins) as non-business 'gifts'.
Root Cause 3

Rate Arbitrage & Inverted Structures

Adjacent rate slabs (5% vs 12% vs 18%) create constant friction on borderline items, alongside blocked refunds on input services.

Telangana Case: M/s Sri Tulasi Industries
Disputes arising from 5% output rate on final goods versus 12% and 18% on input procurements, trapping massive working capital in credit ledgers.
Root Cause 4

Procedural & Jurisdictional Vacuum

Premature Show-Cause Notices on automated system flags (GSTR-2B vs 3B) without consultation, and lack of NAAAR harmonization.

National Friction: Conflicting State AARs
Identical commercial services receive diametrically opposite rulings in Telangana, Karnataka, and Maharashtra, forcing writ petitions in High Courts.

Actionable Policy Blueprint for Revenue Secretary

Four high-leverage administrative and legislative reforms to eliminate over 50% of recurring disputes

Immediate Reform (30 Days)

Pre-SCN Electronic Consultation

Enforce a mandatory 30-day pre-notice automated clarification window on the GST portal for return discrepancies before Sec 73/74 notices are issued.

Administrative Action

Operationalize NAAAR

Formally operationalize the National Appellate Authority for Advance Rulings under Sec 101A–101C to resolve cross-state divergence at inception.

Clarificatory Circulars

Binding Safe-Harbour Matrices

Issue standardized positive/negative tables on common friction points: commercial trade incentives vs gifts, pure agents, and RCM on directors.

GST Council Agenda

Collapse the 12% Rate Slab

Rationalize rate brackets by collapsing 12% into 5% and 18%, eradicating the primary engine of both classification arbitrage and inverted duty refunds.
